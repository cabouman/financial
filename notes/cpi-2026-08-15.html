July CPI: a Second Quiet Month, and the September Hike Looks Less Likely

The July 2026 Consumer Price Index, released Wednesday by the BLS, rose 0.1% on the month. Annual inflation edged down to 3.4% from June's 3.5%, and every headline and core reading matched the Dow Jones consensus forecast.

The month-by-month picture

What happened

July's price increase was small, and it came in exactly where forecasters expected. Shelter rose 0.1% and still accounted for about two-thirds of the monthly headline increase, with owners' equivalent rent up 0.3% and lodging away from home down 2.8%. Energy fell 1.5% on the month after June's 5.7% drop. Even after two down months, energy prices remain 14.7% above a year ago, with gasoline up 24.6% — the residue of the March–May surge that followed the attacks on Iran.

Core inflation was the steadier story. The core index rose 0.2% in July after being unchanged in June, and the annual core rate fell to 2.5% from 2.6%. Within core, airline fares rose 2.2% on the month and medical care rose 0.4%, while motor vehicle insurance fell 0.3%.

The Fed picture

A September rate hike now looks less likely than it did a month ago. At its July meeting the Federal Open Market Committee held rates steady on a 9–3 vote, with all three dissenters preferring a hike. Since then, the July jobs report showed a net loss of jobs, and this CPI report added a second consecutive month of small price increases. Futures markets priced the probability of a September hike at 42% after the release, down from a strong likelihood a week earlier, with more weight now on a move in October or December. Ellen Zentner of Morgan Stanley Wealth Management said that unless the next round of data changes the story, "the Fed will likely still be in a position to leave rates unchanged."

The inflation problem is smaller than it looked in May, but it is not resolved. Headline inflation at 3.4% remains well above the 2% target, and the energy prices that drove the spring surge still depend on conditions in the Middle East. The next CPI release, covering August, arrives September 11 — before the FOMC meets.

Portfolio relevance

For the stock/bond/cash question tracked in the portfolio analysis, this report reduces near-term rate risk without changing the regime question. Two quiet months make an imminent hike less likely, which markets treated as good news for both stocks and bonds: stocks rose and Treasury yields fell on the day of the release, and the S&P 500 reached a record close two days later. The June note's caution still applies. When inflation shocks dominate, stocks and bonds fall together and cash is the safer defensive asset; when growth shocks dominate, bonds are the hedge. One in-line report shifts the odds toward the calmer case but does not settle it.
